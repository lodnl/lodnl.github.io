**Abstract.** Bitcoin divides public opinion about its usefulness. Having in the end of 2024 reached the symbolic threshold of $100,000, an increasing number of investors are considering it as an investment and diversification tool. In this paper, we analyse the temporal evolution of interactions between Bitcoin and international equity markets to attempt to construct an investment solution based on a basket of equities that replicates Bitcoin’s performance. To this end, we first use the connectedness measures developed by Diebold and Yilmaz (2009) to quantify the global interdependencies between Bitcoin and the equity markets. Second, we move to the micro level and attempt to construct a portfolio of individual stocks by minimising the tracking error risk introduced by Roll (1992) and Markowitz (1952). Our data cover three geographic regions (US, Europe, Asia) and span the period from 31 December 2011 to 31 December 2024. Our results show a marked temporal evolution in the relationships between Bitcoin and the equity markets, with a structural break occurring in 2020. Bitcoin appears as a net receiver of shocks and exhibits closer links with US markets than with Europe and Asia. At the sectoral level, the Technology and Financials sectors are the most strongly correlated with Bitcoin. The replication portfolios constructed achieve, after 2020, a correlation of approximately 80% with Bitcoin, underscoring the feasibility of an equity-based trend-following strategy.
